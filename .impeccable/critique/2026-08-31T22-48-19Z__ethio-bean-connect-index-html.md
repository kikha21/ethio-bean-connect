## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored copy on a category-interchangeable chassis.** The words are Ethiopian coffee. The design is a dark B2B template.

Authored and uncounterfeitable: the Origins section trades in specifics an insider would check ("Gedeo zone · 1,800 to 2,200 m", "G2, some G1", "sometimes called Lekempti on paperwork"). The three walls name the real failure modes of the trade. The lot slip with its perforated tear edge and press-and-hold seal is a genuine product metaphor.

Generic: the visual system is the stock premium-dark-SaaS kit (near-black canvas, one gold accent, 16px rounded cards, hairline borders, translateY(-4px) hover, mono uppercase kickers). Swap the words and it sells crypto. **The brand declares three colours and the site uses one:** `--green:#3C8342` and `--bean:#7A5233` are declared then used essentially nowhere; every button, kicker, chip, icon, border, seal and thread is gold. The hero image contains no coffee, no cherries, no drying beds, no people, and is held for 520vh. The one attempted signature, the gold origin thread, is generated by `rng(5)`: a literally random wandering line, shaped by nothing about coffee, that on mobile crosses body copy and form fields.

## Overall Impression

The writing is the best thing here and it is genuinely good: restrained, specific, and credible to someone who has handled the paperwork. The design underneath it is a competent template that contributes none of that specificity, and the conversion path it wraps is broken in ways that would cost real leads. The single biggest opportunity is not visual: it is that the form currently tells people their message was received when it was not.

## What's Working

1. **The Origins section.** It trades in checkable specifics rather than adjectives. "Sometimes called Lekempti on paperwork" is knowledge you only have if you have handled the documents; a washing-station owner knows within one line they are not reading a brochure.
2. **The `Other, let me type it` escape on all three selects.** It removes the commonest reason a real supplier abandons a form, auto-focuses the free-text box, and the reassurance bullet names it before the user hits the wall rather than after.
3. **The copy's refusal to oversell.** "These are indicative levels, not a quote." "Nothing to post a lot, nothing to look." For a second-language audience deciding whether to trust a stranger with a container, restraint reads as competence.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The form lies, in two independent ways

**What.** (a) The submit path calls `window.open(...)` then *unconditionally* hides the form and shows "Got it. We will come back to you within one working day." Verified: with `window.open` returning null — blocked popup, WhatsApp in-app browser, WhatsApp not installed — the success panel still shows, and the form is gone with no way back. (b) `setMode` is bound to `.tabs button`, which also matches the two *route* buttons. Verified: click "I need coffee" then "Email", and the enquiry sends headed `I HAVE COFFEE`.

**Why it matters.** Exporters are the paying side. An exporter who picks "I need coffee" and Email sends the opposite message, with an empty lot description, then reads a confirmation that nothing arrived. On a brokerage whose only asset is trust, the first interaction teaches the visitor the site lies.

**Fix.** Scope the binding to `.tabs button[data-mode]`. Check `window.open`'s return; on null keep the form mounted and render the composed message as selectable text with tappable `wa.me` and `mailto:` links. Rewrite the success copy to describe what actually happened, and add "Edit and send again" that restores the form with values intact.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The primary call to action is unreadable

**What.** Detector-only find; the design review missed it and so did I. `.nav-links a` (specificity 0,1,1) sets `color:var(--text-secondary)` and overrides `.btn-primary` (0,1,0) `color:#14180F`. The nav's "Post your lot" pill therefore renders pale sage on gold: **1.24:1** measured. Hover 1.60:1. Below 860px, 2.08:1. Floor is 4.5:1. The other three `.btn-primary` instances are correct at 7.59:1, which is why it survives a visual check.

**Why it matters.** It is the most important element on the page and its label is effectively invisible in every state.

**Fix.** Scope the nav link colour to `.nav-links a:not(.btn)`, or set `.nav-links a.btn-primary{color:#14180F}`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

### [P1] No error messages exist, and no validation beyond "not empty"

**What.** `novalidate` disables native constraints and `firstInvalid()` tests only for emptiness. Verified: `not-an-email` and `abc` both submit. On empty submit the sole feedback is an orange border plus a focus move: no text, `aria-invalid` null, zero `role="alert"`, nothing announced, exactly one field revealed per attempt.

**Why it matters.** Colour as the only error signal fails WCAG 1.4.1 and says nothing to a second-language reader. Seven required fields revealed one submit at a time is seven round trips. A typo'd email is a lead the broker cannot answer.

**Fix.** Real validation, a message under each invalid field, `aria-invalid` and `aria-describedby`, all seven marked at once with a `role="alert"` summary. Add `inputmode="tel"`. Mark required fields; currently only the two *optional* ones are labelled, which reads as "everything is optional."

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] Mobile loses the column the section exists to show, and loses the CTA

**What.** `table{min-width:600px}` inside `overflow-x:auto`. Measured at 375px: scrollWidth 600 / clientWidth 344, so **256px is hidden, the entire price column**, with no fade, arrow or visible scrollbar. Separately `.nav .btn-primary{display:none}` below 860px removes "Post your lot" from the nav, so for roughly 10,000px of scrolling the only fixed control is the chat button, which opens contact links rather than the form.

**Why it matters.** The section is headed "What lots are moving at." and the column answering it is off-screen; a supplier concludes there is no pricing. Meanwhile the page's one action is unreachable on the device this audience uses.

**Fix.** Below 720px, stack each row as a card with Origin as heading and Grade / Process / Level as label-value pairs; the Origins list already proves the pattern. Add a sticky bottom bar carrying "Post your lot" and fold the chat button into it.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P1] The page ships invisible and depends entirely on JavaScript

**What.** `.rise{opacity:0}` on 38 elements, `.band{opacity:0}` on the hero captions, the hero image applied by JS, and the market rows, origins list, footer contacts and chat launcher all JS-generated into empty containers. Verified: **zero `<noscript>` elements**.

**Why it matters.** One JS error, a blocked script, an older Android WebView, or a proxy stripping inline script, and a supplier in Addis sees a dark rectangle with a logo. For an audience on constrained networks and older devices this is not hypothetical.

**Fix.** Invert the default: ship visible, add the hidden state from JS on boot via a `.js` class. Put the hero poster in CSS. Render the market table and origins list as static HTML that JS enhances rather than creates.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

## False Positives

The detector's single *degraded* finding was its only wrong one: `side-tab` on the origins pull-quote is a 2px aside rule with `border-radius:0 12px 12px 0`, not the thick slab the rule hunts. The un-degraded parser correctly dropped it. Also discounted: `cramped-padding` on `section.alt` (padding is 115.2px vertical; the horizontal inset comes from the child shell), on `.tablewrap` (padding:0 is correct for a scroll container), and on `.faq`; and `div.poster` overflow at 1280px, which is an absolutely-positioned decorative backdrop clipped by its ancestor. The two `clipped-overflow-container` hits are accurate and intentional, but worth keeping in mind: that clip is what guarantees the "no horizontal scroll" result, so it would also mask a real overflow bug.

## Questions to Consider

1. The brand has three colours and the site uses one. What does this page look like if green carries "checked and sealed", brown carries the physical coffee, and gold is reserved for the deal alone?
2. The hero is a misty forest with no coffee, no people and no washing station in it, held for five screens. What if the first thing a visitor saw were a cupping table, a drying bed, or a hand in a jute sack, and the headline landed in one screen instead of five?
3. The seal is the only moment where the visitor does the thing the business does. What if posting a lot *were* sealing one, and the slip filled itself in live as you typed?
4. Every reassurance here is a claim the site makes about itself. For a brokerage asking someone to hand over 30 tonnes and a phone number, what would you have to *show* — a name, a face, a registration number, one completed deal — before the words start counting?
